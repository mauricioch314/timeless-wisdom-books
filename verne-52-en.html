A Drama in Livonia

Published in 1904, A Drama in Livonia (original French: Un Drame en Livonie) transports Jules Verne's readers to the Baltic region, where a murder case becomes a crucible for the ethnic and social tensions simmering beneath the surface of a divided land. When a prominent banker is found dead under suspicious circumstances, the investigation quickly becomes entangled with the prejudices and loyalties that define Livonian society at the turn of the century.

Suspicion falls upon Frankovitch, a man whose reputation and circumstances make him a convenient scapegoat. His arrest divides the community: some see the hand of justice at work, while others recognize the shape of a conspiracy built on old grudges and ethnic hostility. Dimitri Nicolev, a lawyer of formidable intelligence and integrity, takes up the defense, knowing that the case is about far more than the guilt or innocence of one man.

As Nicolev delves deeper, he uncovers a tangle of jealousy, financial interest, and historical resentment that reaches far beyond the murder itself. Johana, Frankovitch's devoted sister, provides the emotional core of the defense, her faith in her brother's innocence unwavering even as the circumstantial evidence mounts. Opposing them is Dr. Poch, the relentless prosecutor, whose confidence in the case against Frankovitch is matched only by his determination to secure a conviction.

Verne, who had explored courtroom drama in earlier novels, here creates his most mature legal thriller — a story in which the search for truth must navigate not only the evidence but the biases and passions that distort it. A Drama in Livonia is a novel about the fragility of justice in a society divided against itself, and about the courage required to stand for truth when the weight of public opinion presses in the opposite direction.
