Mathias Sandorf

Published in 1885, Mathias Sandorf is Jules Verne's most ambitious novel of intrigue and vengeance — a direct homage to Alexandre Dumas's The Count of Monte Cristo. Spanning the entire Mediterranean, it is a story of betrayal, escape, and calculated retribution.

Mathias Sandorf, a Hungarian patriot fighting for his country's independence, is betrayed by those he trusted and sentenced to death. He escapes from the fortress of Pisino with the help of Dr. Bathory, a fellow prisoner, and his loyal co-conspirator Ladislas Zathmar. Presumed dead, Sandorf adopts multiple identities and moves through the Mediterranean world like a shadow, patiently assembling the threads of a plan that will bring his enemies to justice.

His quest takes him from the Adriatic coast to the shores of North Africa, from the salons of Europe to the lawless edges of the sea. Along the way, he encounters Sarcany, a villain of almost theatrical cruelty, and must navigate a web of deception where no one is quite who they appear to be. The novel's intricate plot involves hidden fortunes, secret messages, and a dazzling denouement that brings all the pieces together.

Verne matches the scale of his inspiration while adding his own signature: detailed geography, scientific curiosity, and a fascination with identity and disguise. Mathias Sandorf is a sprawling Mediterranean epic — a tale of justice served cold, of a man who loses everything and rebuilds himself from the ashes of his former life.
