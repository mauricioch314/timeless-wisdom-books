The Giant Raft

Published in 1881, The Giant Raft (also known as The Jangada) transports the reader to the heart of the Amazon rainforest. It is a novel of journey and justice, family and honor, set against the monumental backdrop of the world's greatest river.

The story follows the Garral family, prosperous Brazilian planters who embark on an extraordinary voyage down the Amazon River on a jangada — an immense raft constructed from the timber of the surrounding forest. Joam Garral, the family patriarch, intends to travel to Belém to see his daughter Minha married to her betrothed, a young physician who has been part of their household. The journey is a celebration, a parade of life on the river, and a chance to showcase the riches of the Amazon.

But beneath the surface of this idyllic voyage lies a shadow. A crime committed long ago, for which an innocent man may have suffered, threatens to shatter the Garral family's peace. An accuser emerges from the past, and Joam finds himself confronting a secret that could cost him everything. As the raft descends the river, the tension between the natural beauty around them and the moral crisis within grows ever deeper.

The Giant Raft is a novel of two currents — one carried by the majestic waters of the Amazon, the other by the currents of justice and truth. Verne fills the story with vivid details of Amazonian flora, fauna, and river life, creating a rich sense of place while weaving a legal and emotional drama that builds toward a powerful resolution.
