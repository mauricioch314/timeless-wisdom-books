Travel Scholarships

Published in 1903, Travel Scholarships (original French: Bourses de voyage) begins as a celebration of educational opportunity and gradually transforms into one of Verne's most suspenseful maritime thrillers. A group of exceptional students from a West Indies school — bright, curious, and ambitious — win a prestigious scholarship that promises a grand educational tour through the Caribbean islands. For Tony Renault, Magnus Anders, Niels Harboe, and their classmates, it is the adventure of a lifetime.

Captain Paxton, a seasoned and honorable mariner, commands the vessel that will carry the young scholars from island to island. The journey begins with all the excitement of discovery: the colors of the Caribbean, the histories of each island, the camaraderie of young men exploring the world. Verne, ever the educator, fills these early chapters with geography, history, and natural description, giving the reader a vivid tour of the Antilles through the eyes of the students.

But the voyage takes a dark turn when the ship falls into the hands of Harry Markel, a remorseless pirate whose cunning and cruelty threaten to turn the educational cruise into a catastrophe. What follows is a battle of wits between the students and their captors, a struggle for survival in which the lessons of the classroom prove unexpectedly vital. Tony Renault emerges as a natural leader, his courage and quick thinking tested in ways no scholarship could have anticipated.

Travel Scholarships is Verne's commentary on education as preparation for life — not only the book learning of the classroom, but the resourcefulness, moral clarity, and bravery that true education ought to cultivate. It is a novel of two journeys: one of discovery, and one of survival, and the students must complete both before they can truly claim to have learned anything at all.
