Hector Servadac

Published in 1877, Hector Servadac is one of Jules Verne's most imaginative and playful novels. It combines astronomical speculation, adventure, and human comedy in a story that takes its characters — and its readers — far beyond the bounds of ordinary experience.

Captain Hector Servadac, a French officer stationed in Algeria, is a man of ambition and vanity, composing verses for a woman he hopes to marry. But his plans are disrupted when a cosmic catastrophe tears a fragment of the Earth — and several of its inhabitants — away from the planet. United in an extraordinary new world, Servadac finds himself alongside a Russian nobleman, his orderly, and a small group of survivors, all forced to navigate an unfamiliar reality.

The novel's charm lies in Verne's ability to merge scientific speculation with character comedy. The introduction of the eccentric astronomer Professor Palmyrin Rosette, who emerges as both a source of knowledge and conflict, adds a memorable dimension to the story. Together, the group must unravel the mystery of their situation and adapt to conditions that defy everything they once knew.

Hector Servadac is a celebration of curiosity and resilience in the face of the unknown. While its premise is extraordinary, its heart is thoroughly human — full of humor, rivalry, and the quiet determination to make sense of a world turned upside down.

Characters

Captain Hector Servadac

French officer

An officer stationed in Algeria who assumes leadership of the survivors after the cosmic catastrophe.

Ben-Zouf

Servadac’s orderly

Loyal, practical, and endlessly humorous. He remains beside Servadac even when their entire world is transformed.

Count Timascheff

Russian nobleman

Owner of the schooner Dobryna. Once Servadac’s rival, he becomes one of his most important allies.

Lieutenant Procope

Sailor and navigator

Commander of the Dobryna and a gifted navigator whose knowledge helps the survivors explore and endure their new world.

Palmyrin Rosette

Astronomer

Brilliant, irascible, and obsessed with his calculations. He discovers the true nature and trajectory of Gallia.

Isac Hakhabut

Merchant

Owner of the tartan Hansa. Even far from Earth, he remains devoted to his goods, accounts, and business.
